Digital Safety AI: building a cybersecurity awareness chatbot

Introduction

Digital assistants powered by generative AI are becoming a common way for people to get guidance and practical advice in everyday language. In this AI Lab, our team used generative AI to design and build Digital Safety AI, a chatbot that teaches everyday internet users the basics of staying safe online.

Description

Digital Safety AI is a custom GPT that explains common online threats, helps users spot phishing and scams, gives advice on creating strong passwords, and promotes responsible technology use. It was built on ChatGPT with custom instructions and user context, then tested against realistic questions.

The bot is deliberately limited to education and awareness. It refuses to give hacking or illegal-access instructions and steers users away from unethical cyber activity.

Objective

Help users build a foundational knowledge of cybersecurity and safe online behavior so they can reduce their risk from common cyber threats, using clear language that beginners can follow.

Process

We first practiced with a base large language model, running the same prompt (“Explain common signs of phishing emails”) several times. Every answer covered the core warning signs, but detail, structure and examples varied between runs. That showed us a general model was useful but too generic, so we built a custom GPT and organized the project around the five stages of design thinking:

  1. EmpathizeIdentified the problems users face online: spotting phishing emails, weak passwords, low awareness of security practices, and unsafe use of public Wi-Fi.
  2. DefineFramed the core problem: most internet users lack the knowledge to recognize and protect themselves from cyber threats.
  3. IdeateCompared four concepts: an online safety adviser, a cybersecurity awareness chatbot, a password-security helper, and a computer-based safety education aid. We chose the awareness chatbot because it best served our goal.
  4. PrototypeConfigured the custom GPT with an objective, capabilities and limits, and added scenario context such as advice for connecting to public Wi-Fi.
  5. TestRan test conversations and refined the instructions based on what we saw.

Test scenarios

“What are some clues to look for in phishing e-mails?”

The bot listed suspicious links, urgent language, unknown senders and requests for personal or confidential information.

“What should I do to make a good password?”

The bot recommended longer passwords, mixing letters, numbers and symbols, and avoiding guessable words or personal details.

Challenges and how we solved them

Challenge

Some answers used technical jargon that beginners found hard to follow.

Solution

Rewrote the instructions to ask for simple language and plain explanations.

Challenge

Some answers gave far more information than the question needed.

Solution

Refined the prompts to ask for brief, focused answers.
